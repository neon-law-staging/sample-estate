# Recorded estate-planning session guide

Confidential attorney work product. For use within the engagement.

This is the script for a recorded interview that collects the client's instructions for a Nevada estate plan: a
revocable living trust, a pour-over will, financial and health-care powers of attorney, and plans for personal property,
digital assets, and incapacity. The recording is raw instruction for drafting. It is not legal advice by itself, and it
signs, funds, or changes no document. After the session, counsel processes the recording as described at the end of this
guide, drafts the documents, and returns them to the client for review before a separate signing.

## Before recording

1. Confirm everyone agrees to recording before starting. Use the Firm's approved private recording and storage tools.
2. Have existing estate documents, an asset list, and current beneficiary and legacy-contact settings available.
3. Ask one question at a time. Answer in ordinary language. Say "Not yet on file" when you do not know.
4. Never provide passwords, private keys, seed phrases, or recovery codes. Describe where recovery instructions live.
5. Keep the recording, transcript, answers, and resulting documents in the private Matter file, outside Git. Do not send
   them to public research tools or services that retain or train on the material.

## How to ask the questions

Ask in the order below. Ask only one numbered question at a time. Pause for the answer. Skip conditional sections that
do not apply. Repeat the asset, beneficiary, account, and person questions for each relevant item. Reuse answers already
confirmed. Say "Not yet on file" for unknowns. Say "Undecided" for choices still open. Say "None" when nothing applies.

Use full legal names when naming people. Collect contact details privately after the session. Keep addresses, account
identifiers, and supporting records in the private Matter file.

Read aloud: "This session records your estate-planning instructions. It is not a will or any other signed document, and
nothing takes effect today. You can pause at any time. You can skip a question. You can correct an answer. We will
review the drafts with you before you sign anything."

## Opening

1. What is today's date?
2. Who is present?
3. What is your full legal name?
4. Where do you live?
5. Where is your permanent home?
6. Do you plan to move?
7. What matters most about this plan?
8. Is there an urgent deadline?
9. Is the onboarding letter signed?
10. What questions do you have about the scope?

## Family

11. Are you married?
12. Do you have a partner?
13. Do you have a marital agreement?
14. Do you have support obligations?
15. Do you have children?
16. Does anyone else depend on you?
17. Do you have pets?

If there are minor children:

18. Who should be their guardian?
19. Who is the backup guardian?

If there are pets:

20. Who should care for them?
21. Who is the backup caregiver?
22. What money should be set aside for their care?

## Existing documents

Check for a will, trust, financial power of attorney, power of attorney for health care, declaration governing
life-sustaining treatment, medical-records authorization, disposition-of-remains affidavit, deed upon death, and funeral
arrangements. Repeat for each existing document.

23. Do you have this document?
24. When was it signed?
25. Where is the original?
26. What would you like to change?

## Personal assets

Cover each applicable category: real estate, bank accounts, investments, retirement accounts, insurance, employer
equity, business interests, vehicles, valuables, sentimental belongings, intellectual property, and cryptocurrency.
Include property outside Nevada. For each asset, ask whether it is community or separate property. Repeat the following
questions for each asset or group of similar belongings.

27. What is the asset?
28. Where is it held?
29. Who is the legal owner?
30. Who else has an ownership interest?
31. What share do you own?
32. What is its approximate value?
33. What is the date of that value?
34. What debt is attached to it?
35. What limits apply to transferring it?
36. What deadline applies to it?
37. Who is its current named beneficiary?
38. Who is its current backup beneficiary?
39. Who should receive it under this plan?
40. What record can confirm ownership?

Then cover debts that are not attached to a listed asset.

41. What other debts do you owe?
42. What obligations have you guaranteed?

## Inheritance

43. Who should receive a specific gift?

For each specific gift:

44. What should this person receive?
45. Who receives the gift if this person dies before you?

Then cover the rest of the estate.

46. Who should inherit the remainder?

For each beneficiary of the remainder:

47. What share should this person receive?
48. Who takes that share if this person dies before you?
49. Should this inheritance be held in trust?
50. When should this person receive it?
51. What conditions should apply?
52. Does this person receive means-tested benefits?
53. What support needs should the plan address?

## Executor

The executor administers the estate under the will. The will calls this role the personal representative.

54. Who should be your executor?
55. Who is the backup executor?

## Trustees

The trustee manages property held in the trust. Name the initial trustee. Name each successor trustee, even one who is
also your executor. Ask each successor question separately.

56. Who should be the initial trustee?
57. Who should serve alongside that trustee, if anyone?
58. Who takes over if you cannot manage the trust?
59. Who takes over after your death?
60. Who is the next backup trustee?
61. How should inability to serve be established?
62. Should trustees be paid?
63. Who should receive trust reports?
64. Who may remove a trustee?
65. Who may appoint a replacement trustee?
66. Which assets should enter the trust?
67. Which assets should stay outside the trust?

If more than one trustee will serve at a time:

68. How should they make decisions?
69. How should they resolve disagreements?

## Digital accounts and existing settings

Cover email, cloud storage, photos, social accounts, domains, websites, code, package registries, devices, backups,
self-hosted services, and digital wallets. Add these to the same asset inventory. Review existing provider settings
before drafting document directions. Repeat for each account.

70. What is the account?
71. Who is the provider?
72. Who owns the account?
73. Does the provider offer legacy settings?
74. Who is currently named in those settings?
75. What do those settings direct?
76. Can you change those directions?
77. Can you delete those directions?
78. When did you last check them?

## Digital permissions

The financial agent acts during life. The executor acts for the estate after death. A trustee acts for the trust. A
permission given to one role does not carry over to another. Repeat these questions for each account where the role
applies.

79. May your financial agent access this account?
80. May your financial agent receive its message content?
81. What limits should apply to that agent?
82. May your executor access this account after death?
83. May your executor receive its message content?
84. What limits should apply to that executor?
85. May your successor trustee access this trust account?
86. May your successor trustee receive its message content?
87. What limits should apply to that trustee?
88. What should happen to this account after death?

For the last question, possible answers include preservation, transfer, memorialization, closure, or deletion. Record
any uncertainty for provider review; do not assume the provider supports the requested action.

## Digital recovery and continuity

Repeat for each relevant digital asset. Skip details already confirmed in the asset inventory.

89. Who should inherit this digital property?
90. Who should maintain it?
91. What employer rights affect it?
92. What co-author rights affect it?
93. What license restrictions affect it?
94. What provider restrictions affect it?
95. When is its next renewal?
96. How is its renewal paid?
97. Who should handle its renewal?
98. What exists only on this device?
99. What is encrypted?
100. Where are its recovery instructions kept?
101. How will the authorized person find those instructions?
102. Have you tested the recovery process yourself?
103. What failed during that test?
104. What remains unresolved?

Never provide passwords, private keys, seed phrases, or recovery codes. Record recovery locations and procedures only.

## Financial power of attorney

105. Who should be your financial agent?
106. Who is the backup agent?
107. When should the agent's authority begin?
108. What should establish any required triggering event?
109. Should the agent be paid?
110. Who should monitor transactions?

If more than one agent will serve at a time:

111. Must they act together?

Repeat the next two questions separately for each power: banking, real estate, personal property, business operations,
taxes, retirement accounts, insurance, trust matters, and digital matters. Reuse the message-content answers above.

112. May your agent exercise this power?
113. What limits should apply to this power?

Then ask about these specific powers:

114. May the agent make gifts?
115. What limits should apply to gifts?
116. May the agent make gifts to themselves?
117. What limits should apply to those gifts?
118. May the agent change beneficiaries?
119. What limits should apply to beneficiary changes?

## Health care

120. Who should make health-care decisions for you?
121. Who is the alternate agent?
122. What limits should apply to the agent's authority?
123. Should the power of attorney for health care expire?
124. What should determine its expiration?
125. Do you want a separate declaration governing life-sustaining treatment?

Discuss each treatment separately, including CPR, a ventilator, artificial nutrition (tube feeding), and artificial
hydration. Ask for wishes in the client's own words. Under a Nevada declaration, tube feeding is withheld at the end of
life unless the client says otherwise in writing, so ask this one expressly.

126. What are your wishes about this treatment?
127. In what circumstances should that instruction apply?

Then cover medical information and donation.

128. Who may receive your medical information?
129. What information should be excluded?
130. When should that authorization expire?
131. What are your organ-donation wishes?
132. What are your eye-donation wishes?
133. What are your tissue-donation wishes?

## Funeral and remains

134. Who should have authority to order the disposition of your remains?
135. Who is the backup?
136. What disposition do you want for your body?
137. Where should that take place?
138. What funeral wishes do you have?
139. What existing funeral contract should we review?

## Confirm the people named

Repeat for each person named to serve in a role. Confirm each role separately when one person has several roles.

140. Has this person agreed to serve in this role?
141. What concern do you have about this appointment?
142. What could prevent this person from serving?

## Confirm the answers

Read back one appointment at a time, including every trustee. Read back one inheritance instruction at a time. Read back
one digital permission at a time, identifying the account and role. After each item, ask:

143. Is that correct?

Then close:

144. What would you like to correct?
145. What have we missed?
146. Which decisions remain open?
147. Which records still need to be provided?

Repeat for each follow-up item:

148. Who will handle this item?
149. When will it be ready?

Finally:

150. How would you like to review drafts?
151. Where will you sign?
152. Who can attend signing?
153. Where should the signed originals be kept?

## Documents these answers support

| Document | Main instructions needed |
| --- | --- |
| Onboarding letter | Client, agreed scope, fees, and engagement status |
| Revocable living trust | Trustees, successors, beneficiaries, distribution terms, and funding choices |
| Certification of trust | Trust name and date, acting trustees, and the powers a third party needs to see |
| Pour-over will | Trust details, executor and backup, guardian nominations if needed, and digital consent |
| Statutory form financial power of attorney | Agents, start time, powers, limits, oversight, and digital consent |
| Durable power of attorney for health care | Agent, alternate, treatment wishes, and limits |
| Life-sustaining treatment declaration, if wanted | Treatment instructions, any designee, and the tube-feeding choice |
| Medical-records authorization | Recipients, information covered, exclusions, and duration |
| Disposition-of-remains affidavit | Designee, successor, and disposition wishes |
| Private asset inventory | Ownership, values, debts, beneficiaries, providers, and supporting records |
| Digital succession instructions | Access choices, recovery locations, maintainers, renewals, and account treatment |
| Funding and beneficiary paperwork | Applicable deeds, specific assignments, account forms, and beneficiary changes |

Nevada supplies statutory forms for the financial power of attorney (NRS 162A.620), the advance health-care directive,
which includes a power of attorney for health care (NRS 162A.855), the declaration (NRS 449A.436 and 449A.439), and the
remains affidavit (NRS 451.024). The trust, the will, and the other documents are drafted to the instructions. Keep the
health-care and financial powers of attorney as separate instruments.

## Instructions for processing the recording

Use only a Firm-approved private transcription and drafting workflow. Preserve the original recording and transcript.

1. Confirm the correct Matter and client before attaching the recording or recording answers. Reuse existing Person
   records for named people; distinguish people with similar names and record each role separately.
2. Extract explicit answers under the sections above. For each decision, retain its timestamp and the client's wording.
   Identify who spoke. A suggested answer from someone else is not the client's instruction unless the client adopts it.
3. Mark missing answers "Not yet on file." Keep questions, tentative preferences, and confirmed instructions distinct.
   Flag contradictions for follow-up. Do not silently choose an answer or infer consent from silence.
4. Produce a role list, inheritance instructions, one asset schedule, and digital permissions by account and role.
   Include a list of missing records and decisions. Distinguish reported ownership from ownership supported by records.
5. Map the answers to the existing combined intake and the document list above. Identify what is ready for drafting and
   what is blocked. Use visible placeholders in drafts for unresolved facts; do not fabricate names, terms, or
   authority.
6. Have counsel review provider settings, ownership and beneficiary conflicts, trust funding, digital consent, and
   signing requirements. The trust must be signed at or before the pour-over will; schedule their execution together.
7. Return the instructions and drafts for client review. Obtain explicit confirmation of corrections before finalizing.
   Schedule signing and track each funding task separately. Recording the session does not sign or fund any document.

## Counsel reference notes

- Trust creation and revocability: NRS 163.002, 163.003 and 163.004.
- Pour-over coordination: NRS 163.230.
- Community property: NRS 123.220; NRS 123.125 (character of property in an irrevocable trust for both spouses).
- Will execution, self-proving affidavit, and interested witnesses: NRS 133.040, 133.050 and 133.060.
- Online-tool directions: NRS 722.310. Agent access to message content: NRS 722.360.
- Financial power of attorney: NRS 162A.220, 162A.450 and 162A.620.
- Health care: NRS 162A.790, 162A.840 and 162A.855; NRS 449A.433 and 449A.451.
- Disposition of remains: NRS 451.024.

These references support preparation and review. Counsel should verify current law and the applicable execution
requirements when preparing final instruments.
